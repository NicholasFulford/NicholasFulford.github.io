Motivated by the growing importance of AI security and interpretability, this project investigates how the topology of an LLM's internal activations changes under adversarial prompt injection attacks, and tests whether such changes distinguish adversarial injection attempts from benign inputs. It builds on <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20435'>Fay et al. (2025)</a>, who used persistent homology to show that adversarial inputs induce "topological compression" in LLM latent spaces. Persistent homology characterises the shape of a representation space, rather than only summary statistics such as means and distances. Here it is applied to point clouds of last-token activations, one point per prompt at a given layer, comparing adversarial injection attempts against length-matched benign insertions, with significant topological features separated from sampling noise by a bootstrap filter.

This project is being carried out in parallel with 'Characterising Regime Change In Financial Markets'.
